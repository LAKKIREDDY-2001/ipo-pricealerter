IPO vs FPO vs OFS — Key Differences Explained for Indian Stock Investors

By Venkata Madhava Reddy (Lead Financial Analyst) • 12 min read • Published: October 2026 • Updated: November 3, 2026

When a corporation raises capital on the Indian stock exchanges, it utilizes various primary market instruments depending on whether the company is unlisted or already actively traded. The three most common equity offerings are Initial Public Offerings (IPO), Follow-on Public Offerings (FPO), and Offers for Sale (OFS).

In this guide, we evaluate the distinct characteristics, valuation impacts, and investor implications of each offering type.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is an Offer for Sale (OFS) on the stock exchange?

An Offer for Sale (OFS) is a streamlined secondary market mechanism that allows promoters of listed companies to dilute their ownership transparently through a dedicated exchange order bidding window, often to comply with SEBI's minimum 25% public shareholding rule.

Does an FPO cause equity dilution for existing shareholders?

Yes. In a dilutive Follow-on Public Offering (FPO), new shares are created, which increases the company's total equity share base and temporarily reduces earnings per share (EPS).

Which offering is safest for retail investors?

Each offering carries different risk profiles. FPOs and OFS transactions involve companies with established trading histories and quarterly audited track records on NSE/BSE, whereas IPOs require evaluating unlisted financial disclosures.
