IPO vs FPO vs OFS — What's the Difference for Retail Investors?

By IPO PriceAlerter Research • 7 min read • Updated: November 2024

When participating in the Indian capital markets, retail investors frequently encounter opportunities to buy shares directly through corporate fundraising routes rather than regular secondary market trading. The three most common corporate actions are IPO (Initial Public Offering), FPO (Follow-on Public Offer), and OFS (Offer for Sale).

While all three mechanisms allow investors to acquire equity shares, their underlying corporate objectives, capital dilution impact, bidding windows, and pricing models differ substantially. Understanding these differences is essential for evaluating risks and potential returns.

1. Initial Public Offering (IPO)

An IPO is the maiden public issue by an unlisted private company. It represents the company's debut on recognized stock exchanges (NSE/BSE). It can consist of a Fresh Issue (infusing capital into the company), an Offer for Sale (letting existing owners liquidate), or a mix of both.

2. Follow-on Public Offer (FPO)

A Follow-on Public Offer (FPO) occurs when a company that is already listed on the stock exchange issues additional fresh shares to the public to raise supplementary expansion capital or repay debts. Because the company is already actively traded, investors have full visibility into historical market prices, quarterly audited balance sheets, and dividend yields.

3. Offer for Sale (OFS)

An Offer for Sale (OFS) is an accelerated exchange bidding mechanism introduced by SEBI to allow listed companies' promoters or institutional shareholders to reduce their stake to comply with the Minimum Public Shareholding (MPS) mandate (25% public float) or achieve disinvestment targets.

Unlike an IPO or FPO, an OFS generates zero new equity shares (no dilution) and raises zero capital for the company. The transactions execute on a dedicated trading window over 1 to 2 business days.

Key Takeaways for Indian Retail Investors

  • Risk Profile: IPOs generally carry the highest volatility because valuation discovery is untested. FPOs and OFSs are anchored to existing live secondary market trading prices, offering greater pricing transparency.
  • Discounts: Promoters frequently offer a 5% discount to retail bidders in OFS transactions to ensure successful fulfillment of public float mandates.
