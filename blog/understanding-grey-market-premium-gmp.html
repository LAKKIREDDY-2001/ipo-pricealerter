Understanding Grey Market Premium (GMP) in IPOs — Realities & Risks

By Venkata Madhava Reddy (Lead Financial Analyst) • 14 min read • Published: October 2026 • Updated: November 3, 2026

Whenever an exciting public issue opens for bidding in the Indian stock market, financial headlines and retail investor forums buzz with one metric above all others: the Grey Market Premium (GMP). Traders discuss figures like "+₹70 GMP (44%)" or "+₹980 GMP (66%)", using these quotes as a crystal ball to predict listing day profits.

However, despite its pervasive influence across social media and financial blogs, the Grey Market Premium is also one of the most misunderstood and speculative metrics in the Indian capital markets. In this guide, we demystify how the grey market functions, how off-market premiums are determined, and why relying blindly on GMP can lead to catastrophic investment losses.

CRITICAL NOTICE: GMP is Unofficial and Speculative

The Grey Market is an informal, unregulated over-the-counter (OTC) market. It has zero legal recognition from SEBI, NSE, or BSE. Published GMP numbers are purely speculative sentiment indicators and provide absolutely no legal or financial guarantee of actual listing day gains.

What is Grey Market Premium (GMP)?

The Grey Market Premium (GMP) is the unofficial cash premium that off-market buyers and private dealers are willing to pay over and above the company's official issue price before the shares officially commence trading on the stock exchange.

Estimated Listing Price = IPO Cap Price + Grey Market Premium (GMP)

For example, if an IPO prices at an upper band of ₹158 and the prevailing Grey Market Premium is reported at +₹70, the estimated off-market debut price is ₹228 per share, translating to an expected listing gain of +44.30%.

The Two Types of Grey Market Contracts: Kostak vs. Subject to Sauda

In the informal dealer networks that operate across major Indian financial hubs like Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Rajkot, and Jaipur, two primary transaction structures exist:

1. Kostak Rate (Application Sale)

The Kostak Rate is the fixed rupee amount an investor receives by selling their entire IPO application to a grey market buyer prior to the announcement of allotment. This payment is guaranteed to the seller regardless of whether shares are ultimately allotted or not. Risk-averse retail bidders use Kostak to lock in a small risk-free gain (e.g., ₹500 to ₹1,000 per application), transferring the lottery risk entirely to the buyer.

2. Subject to Sauda (Allotment Dependent)

Under a Subject to Sauda agreement, the transaction is executed only if the seller successfully wins an allotment in the computerized registrar lottery. If no allotment is received, the contract is automatically voided with zero financial exchange. If allotted, the seller is obligated to hand over the listing day profit to the buyer in exchange for the agreed premium.

Key Drivers Influencing Grey Market Premiums

Grey market sentiment is driven by a confluence of fundamental and technical market forces:

  • Institutional & QIB Subscription Multiples: When marquee mutual funds, foreign portfolio investors (FPIs), and domestic institutions bid aggressively (>50x) in the QIB category, grey market premiums often surge due to perceived scarcity.
  • Anchor Investor Pedigree: The presence of sovereign wealth funds (e.g., GIC, ADIA) and reputable domestic asset managers in the anchor book instills immense confidence.
  • Broad Market Volatility (Nifty & Sensex Trend): If benchmark indices experience a sharp multi-day correction between issue closure and listing day, GMP numbers frequently collapse in tandem.
  • Sector Tailwinds & Theme Hype: Companies operating in booming sectors (solar energy, defense manufacturing, electronics EMS, quick-commerce) naturally command richer grey market valuations.

The Perils of Artificial GMP Rigging (Especially in SME Issues)

Because the grey market lacks electronic order books, public audit trails, and regulatory surveillance, market cartels and unscrupulous promoters frequently engage in artificial premium inflation:

  • Circular Trading: A small group of dealers trade low quantities among themselves at elevated prices to print artificial quotes on social media channels.
  • Trapping Retail Bidders: Lured by tempting +50% to +100% GMP quotes, thousands of retail investors apply without reviewing audited financials.
  • The Post-Closure Evaporation: Once the bidding window closes at 5:00 PM, the artificial buying support vanishes. On listing day, the stock debuts at a steep discount, leaving retail investors trapped in illiquid holdings.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is trading in the grey market legal under Indian securities law?

Grey market trading is completely unofficial, informal, and unregulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). Contracts executed in the grey market are legally unenforceable in Indian courts. If a counterparty defaults on payment, SEBI and stock exchanges provide zero dispute resolution or investor protection.

Can GMP turn negative before listing day?

Yes. If market sentiment deteriorates, if the anchor book exhibits high selling pressure, or if broader equity indices crash, an IPO's GMP can drop below zero (discount). A negative GMP indicates that dealers expect the stock to list below its issue price.

Should I base my IPO investment decision solely on GMP?

Absolutely not. Professional institutional investors evaluate company fundamentals: 3-year revenue CAGR, operating profit margins (EBITDA), promoter integrity, debt-to-equity ratios, and peer valuation comparisons rather than relying on unofficial grey market estimates.
