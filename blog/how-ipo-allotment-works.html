How IPO Allotment Works in India — Retail Quota & Lottery System Explained

By Venkata Madhava Reddy (Lead Financial Analyst) • 13 min read • Published: October 2026 • Updated: November 3, 2026

Every time a high-profile Initial Public Offering closes with staggering subscription figures—such as 40x, 70x, or exceeding 100x—the single most pervasive question asked across millions of retail investor households is: "How are shares actually allotted, and why didn't I receive a single lot despite bidding within the first ten minutes on Day 1?"

The share allotment process in India is neither arbitrary nor influenced by broker relationships. It is governed by mathematically precise regulations established by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), purposefully designed to maximize the total number of unique individual retail citizens who receive shares.

The Foundational Principle: One Person, One Lot Minimum

Under SEBI regulations, in any oversubscribed retail public issue, no single applicant can receive two lots before every valid applicant receives at least one lot. When retail applications exceed available lots, allotment is conducted via an audited, computerized randomized lottery.

The Mathematical Reality of Retail Bidding (The 1-Lot Strategy)

A common misconception among beginner investors is that applying for the maximum allowable retail limit (e.g., 13 lots or approx ₹1,95,000) increases their probability of being selected in the lottery. Under SEBI guidelines, this is mathematically false whenever the retail portion is oversubscribed:

Suppose an IPO has 1,00,000 retail lots available, and 10,00,000 valid applications are received (10x retail oversubscription). Because the number of unique applicants (10 Lakhs) is ten times higher than the total available lots (1 Lakh), each valid PAN card is entered into the computerized lottery exactly once, regardless of whether that PAN bid for 1 lot or 13 lots.

The 5 Stages of the Allotment Process Behind the Scenes

  1. Bid Cleansing & Technical Rejection: The designated registrar (Link Intime, KFintech, Bigshare) collates electronic bid files from NSE and BSE order books. They run algorithmic filters to reject invalid entries: multiple bids under the same PAN, bids below the cut-off price, or bids with unapproved UPI mandates.
  2. Calculation of Over-Subscription Multiples: The registrar calculates exact ratios by dividing total valid applications by available retail lots.
  3. Executing the Randomized Computerized Lottery: Under the supervision of stock exchange officials and independent chartered accountants, a certified random-number software generator draws winning application numbers. Each selected applicant receives one minimum lot.
  4. Publishing the Official Basis of Allotment: The registrar files the official Basis of Allotment (BOA) report on BSE and NSE disclosures, outlining allotment ratios across Retail, Small NII, Big NII, and employee quotas.
  5. Initiating Bank Debits and Lien Releases: Banks debit the blocked funds for successful winners and immediately unblock the ASBA lien for non-allotted participants.

Understanding the Official Basis of Allotment Document (BOA)

Every public issue results in a publicly accessible legal document called the Basis of Allotment, hosted on the BSE India and registrar portals. This document outlines the exact allotment ratio for each category. For example, a ratio of 1:7 in the retail category signifies that for every 7 valid applications received, exactly 1 applicant was picked in the randomized draw.

In the Small NII category (sNII), allotments are also executed via randomized draw for the minimum lot size (valued at over ₹2 Lakhs). In the Big NII category (bNII), after allocating the minimum lot to lucky lottery winners, any surplus shares are distributed proportionally among eligible bidders.

Five Debunked Myths Regarding IPO Allotment

  • Myth 1: "Applying at 10:00 AM on Day 1 increases chances." Truth: All valid applications confirmed before 5:00 PM on closing day enter the lottery with equal probability. Timestamp within the window is completely irrelevant.
  • Myth 2: "Applying through a large national bank gives priority over discount brokers." Truth: Registrars verify bids directly against the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and BSE consolidated order book, not the front-end broker application.
  • Myth 3: "Bidding maximum lots guarantees at least one lot." Truth: In an oversubscribed retail issue, bidding for 13 lots results in either 1 lot (if drawn in lottery) or 0 lots. It never guarantees allotment.
  • Myth 4: "Submitting multiple bids from different brokers under my PAN doubles my odds." Truth: Registrars use PAN as the primary deduplication key. Submitting multiple bids under one PAN results in immediate disqualification of all your bids.
  • Myth 5: "UPI ASBA bids have lower priority than Net Banking ASBA." Truth: Exchange matching engines treat UPI ASBA and Net Banking ASBA orders identically.

Legitimate Strategies to Improve Allotment Chances

  • Apply Across Multiple Family Accounts: Opening Demat accounts for parents, spouse, or adult children and submitting 1 lot per PAN multiplies your lottery entries without locking up excessive capital.
  • Always Check the Cut-Off Price Box: If an issue prices at the ceiling (e.g. ₹158) and you placed a bid at ₹150, your bid is disqualified before the lottery even begins.
  • Avoid Last-Minute Mandate Delays: Approve UPI mandates well before 4:00 PM on the closing day to avoid payment gateway server timeouts.
  • Track Shareholder Quotas: If you hold even 1 share of a listed parent company (e.g. holding Tata Motors before Tata Tech IPO), you can legally apply in both the Retail quota AND the Shareholder quota from the same PAN, doubling your legitimate bidding routes.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does applying on Day 1 give a higher allotment chance than Day 3?

No. The allotment lottery treats all valid electronic applications identically, whether submitted on the first minute of Day 1 or the last hour of Day 3. Timing within the official bidding window has zero impact on lottery odds.

Can I submit two applications from two different broker accounts using the same PAN?

No. If you submit multiple bids under the same Permanent Account Number (PAN)—even across different brokers like Zerodha and Groww—the registrar's data validation filter detects the duplicate PAN and automatically rejects all your applications as invalid.

How is allotment conducted in the HNI / NII category?

The Non-Institutional Investor (NII) category is divided into Small NII (sNII, ₹2L–₹10L) and Big NII (bNII, >₹10L). In oversubscribed issues, allotment is also determined via randomized computerized lottery where winning applicants receive the minimum NII lot value.
