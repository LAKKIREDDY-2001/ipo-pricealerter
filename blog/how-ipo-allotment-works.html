How IPO Allotment Works in India — Retail Quota & Lottery System Explained

By IPO PriceAlerter Research • 8 min read • Updated: November 2024

Every time a high-profile IPO closes with massive subscription numbers—such as 50x, 70x, or over 100x—the most common question asked by millions of retail investors is: "How are shares actually allotted, and why didn't I get a single lot despite applying on day one?"

The share allotment process in India is not arbitrary. It is governed by rigorous, mathematically defined SEBI regulations designed to prioritize equitable distribution among the maximum number of individual citizens. Here is how the allotment machinery works behind the scenes.

The SEBI Retail Allotment Rule: One Person, One Lot Minimum

Under SEBI's standardized allotment guidelines for oversubscribed retail issues, the fundamental principle is that no retail applicant shall receive two lots before every valid applicant receives at least one lot.

When an issue is oversubscribed in the retail category (which happens in almost all popular IPOs), the total number of retail applicants exceeds the total number of minimum lots available in the retail quota. In this scenario, allocating shares proportionally to bid sizes is impossible. Instead, SEBI mandates a computerized randomized lottery.

The Mathematical Reality of Retail Bidding

Applying for 13 lots (₹1.95 Lakhs) in the retail category gives you the exact same mathematical probability of allotment as applying for just 1 lot (₹15,000) whenever the retail category is oversubscribed. Extra lots in the retail quota do not increase your lottery ticket count under SEBI rules.

Step-by-Step Breakdown of the Allotment Process

  1. Data Cleansing & Technical Rejection: The designated registrar (Link Intime, KFintech, Bigshare) collates all electronic bids from NSE and BSE order books. They run algorithmic filters to reject invalid applications: duplicate bids under the same PAN, bids below the cut-off price, and bids where UPI mandates were not approved before 5:00 PM.
  2. Calculating the Over-Subscription Factor: Total valid retail applications are compared with the total number of retail lots available. If 10,00,000 valid applications are received for 1,00,000 available lots, the retail subscription is exactly 10x oversubscribed.
  3. Executing the Randomized Lottery Draw: Under official supervision from exchange representatives and independent auditors, an automated software script generates randomized seed numbers to select the lucky 1,00,000 winning applicants. Each winner is allotted exactly one minimum lot.
  4. Preparation of Basis of Allotment Document: The registrar publishes the formal Basis of Allotment document detailing exact ratios for retail, sNII, bNII, and employee quotas.
  5. Fund Debit & Unblock Instructions: The registrar sends clearing instructions to banks. For allotted investors, the blocked ASBA amount is debited. For non-allotted applicants, the bank unblocks the lien.

How HNI / NII Allotment Works

Unlike the Retail quota, the Non-Institutional Investor (NII) category is divided into two distinct sub-categories with different rules:

  • Small NII (sNII - ₹2 Lakh to ₹10 Lakh): Allotment follows a computerized lottery system similar to retail, with winners receiving the minimum sNII lot value (approx ₹2 Lakh+).
  • Big NII (bNII - >₹10 Lakh): In oversubscribed issues, allotment is also conducted via randomized draw for the minimum bNII lot allotment.

Legitimate Strategies to Improve Your Allotment Chances

  • Apply Under Multiple Family Accounts: Since retail allotment is one lot per PAN in oversubscribed issues, applying for 1 lot each from 4 separate Demat accounts (family members) gives you 4 distinct lottery tickets, quadrupling your probability compared to bidding 4 lots from a single account.
  • Always Choose the Cut-Off Price: If you bid at the lower end of the price band and the final price is fixed at the upper ceiling, your bid is immediately disqualified from the lottery.
  • Ensure Timely Mandate Approval: Complete your UPI mandate approval well before the final closing hour to prevent technical bank network timeouts.
