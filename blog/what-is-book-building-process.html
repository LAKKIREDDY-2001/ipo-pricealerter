What is the Book Building Process in an IPO? Price Discovery Explained

By IPO Pricealerter Research Team Published: 05 November 2024 Updated: 15 November 2024 Reading Time: 8 mins

When a private company decides to go public in India, determining the fair valuation of its shares is one of the most critical challenges. If the price is set too high, the issue risks failing due to inadequate demand; if it is set too low, the company leaves substantial capital on the table.

Historically, companies used fixed-price issues where a single rigid price was determined beforehand. Today, over 95% of mainboard issues utilize the Book Building Process—a dynamic, demand-driven price discovery mechanism regulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI).

Understanding the Book Building Mechanism

Book building is essentially an auction process through which an issuing company attempts to discover the true market value of its equity shares. Instead of setting a single fixed price, the issuer, in consultation with its appointed Book Running Lead Managers (BRLMs) or investment bankers, announces a Price Band.

During the public bidding period (typically 3 working days), institutional, high-net-worth, and retail investors submit bids indicating the quantity of shares they are willing to purchase at various price points within that band. These orders are recorded in a centralized electronic order book maintained by stock exchanges (NSE and BSE).

Core Anatomy of an IPO Price Band

Under SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018, every price band features strict statutory parameters:

  • Floor Price: The lower threshold of the price band (e.g., ₹285). Investors cannot bid below this level.
  • Cap Price: The upper threshold of the price band (e.g., ₹300). Under SEBI guidelines, the cap price cannot exceed 120% of the floor price (meaning the spread between floor and cap cannot exceed 20%).
  • Cut-Off Price: The final clearing price determined by the issuer and lead managers at which shares are issued to eligible investors.

💡 What is the "Cut-Off Price" Option for Retail Investors?

SEBI permits Retail Individual Investors (RIIs bidding up to ₹2,00,000) to select the "Cut-Off" checkbox. Selecting cut-off means: "I agree to purchase the allotted shares at whatever final price is discovered by the company." This shields retail investors from having their applications rejected if the issue clears at the cap price. High Net-Worth Individuals (HNIs/NIIs) and institutional bidders are not legally allowed to bid at cut-off; they must specify an exact limit price.

The 5 Stages of the Book Building Lifecycle

  1. Lead Manager Appointment & Due Diligence: The issuer hires merchant bankers (BRLMs) who conduct legal, financial, and business audits, helping formulate the initial price band based on peer valuation multiples and discounted cash flow models.
  2. Filing of the Red Herring Prospectus (RHP): The company files the RHP with the Registrar of Companies (RoC) and SEBI. The RHP contains all complete operational and financial disclosures, but leaves the final issue price and exact share count blank until the book is closed.
  3. Anchor Investor Bidding (T-1 Day): One day prior to public opening, Qualified Institutional Buyers bid for the Anchor Quota at a fixed price to anchor investor confidence.
  4. Public Bidding Window (3 Days): Bids pour in across categories—Retail (RII), Non-Institutional (NII), and QIB. Real-time subscription tallies are broadcasted live on exchange terminals.
  5. Price Discovery & Basis of Allotment: Once the book closes, the BRLMs analyze the demand curve. The price point at which aggregate demand satisfies the total offered supply is designated as the Issue Price.

Book Building vs. Fixed Price: Key Differences

Feature Book Building Issue Fixed Price Issue
Pricing Discovered dynamically within a 20% price band Pre-determined by company before issue opens
Price Awareness Investors know only the range (Floor and Cap) Investors know exact share price in advance
Demand Visibility Demand book visible live throughout the 3-day bidding window Demand only calculated after closure of subscription
Regulatory Standard Mandatory for almost all modern mainboard IPOs Used occasionally for micro-cap SME offerings
Retail Cut-off Option Available for retail category (≤₹2 Lakh) Not applicable (price is already fixed)

Why Almost Every Major IPO Uses Book Building

Book building represents a win-win market compromise:

  • For Issuing Companies: Ensures capital is raised at the highest clearing price that the open market will bear, preventing under-pricing.
  • For Investors: Prevents aggressive promoter overpricing because if institutional investors find the valuation unattractive, their refusal to bid forces the issuer to lower the issue price toward the floor band.
