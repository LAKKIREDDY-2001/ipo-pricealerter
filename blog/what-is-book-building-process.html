What is the Book Building Process in an IPO? Price Discovery Explained

By Venkata Madhava Reddy (Lead Financial Analyst) • 12 min read • Published: October 2026 • Updated: November 3, 2026

In modern financial markets, companies rarely price their public offerings using a rigid fixed number. Instead, they employ an dynamic market-driven mechanism known as the Book Building Process to discover the optimal clearing price for their equity shares.

In this guide, we demystify the mechanics of book building, the role of Book Running Lead Managers (BRLMs), and how price bands guide institutional and retail bidding.

What is the Book Building Process?

Book building is a price discovery mechanism where the issuing company offers a Price Band (a price floor and a price ceiling) and invites bids from institutional, HNI, and retail investors across three business days. An electronic "book" of demand is compiled, allowing the company and its merchant bankers to determine the final issue price based on actual market demand.

The 20% Price Band Rule Mandated by SEBI

Under SEBI ICDR (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) regulations, the spread between the floor price and the cap price cannot exceed 20%. For instance, if the floor price is ₹100, the cap price cannot exceed ₹120.

The Role of Anchor Investors

Up to 60% of the Qualified Institutional Buyer (QIB) portion can be allocated to Anchor Investors one business day prior to the public bidding launch. Anchor investors must bid at a fixed price and are subject to mandatory lock-in periods (50% locked for 30 days, remaining 50% locked for 90 days), providing market stability and anchor pricing confidence.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between Book Building and a Fixed Price Issue?

In a Fixed Price Issue, the company fixes one single immutable price (e.g., ₹100) before opening. In a Book Built Issue, a 20% price band (e.g., ₹90 to ₹108) is announced, and the final price is discovered dynamically based on investor demand curves.

Who manages the electronic bidding book during an IPO?

Book Running Lead Managers (BRLMs) and merchant bankers (such as Kotak Capital, Morgan Stanley, ICICI Securities, Axis Capital) manage the book under exchange supervision.

What is the lock-in period for anchor investors in Indian IPOs?

Under current SEBI guidelines, anchor investors are subject to a dual-tranche lock-in: 50% of their allocated shares are locked for 30 days from listing, while the remaining 50% are locked for 90 days.
